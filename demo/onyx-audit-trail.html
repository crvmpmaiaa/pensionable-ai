Necessity, then invention.

The workbook's calculation roots include WTW/BCUK modules we didn't have local access to during this engagement. Where those modules are called from the workbook's formula graph, the cells return #NAME? unless a compatible Python stand-in is in place. We used what we did have Pensionable's existing calculation stack, the scheme rules, the trust deed, and the published Revaluation, GMP Increase, and Section 148 Orders from legislation.gov.uk and rebuilt the equivalent calculation logic in Python from those same statutory sources.

Cached workbook outputs and freshly-recalculated workbook values are validation anchors against that independent reconstruction. Where the workbook can be recalculated through our Python stand-ins, it agrees with the engine see the test results below.

How this works

The workbook validates the engine. The rules authorise it.

The Onyx workbook stores many outputs as cached values from BCUK calculation modules. With those modules unavailable locally, live recalculation breaks at the _xll.tk_* calls and the downstream formula graph errors. The engine is therefore derived independently from scheme rules, UK pensions law, published statutory tables, workbook formula shape, and mined empirical tk_* pairs. Workbook agreement is validation, not the method source.

Scheme rules

  • D1.2 for deferred-pension parts and excess revaluation.
  • D2.1, D3, and H3.2 for Early/Late timing and actuarial value.
  • E2, E3, E6, and E8 for dependant construction and limitations.

Legislation

  • PSA 1993 Sch 3, s.84, s.109, and s.110.
  • SSPA 1975 s.17 and s.36.
  • PA 2008 and contracted-out amendment regulations.

Data tables

  • 15 Revaluation Order SIs from legislation.gov.uk.
  • Pensions Increase, Section 148, and GAD fixed-rate tables.
  • Scenario factor rows are now derived first-principles from Parameters and Factors!L5:O15 + R5:AJ16; the bundled CSV is retained as a regression fixture.
